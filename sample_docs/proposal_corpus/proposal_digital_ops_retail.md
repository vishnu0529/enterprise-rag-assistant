## Understanding of the requirement

The client, a UK homeware retailer trading from 34 stores plus a growing
online channel, was averaging a 6.2-day order-to-fulfilment cycle against a
sector benchmark closer to 4 days, with an estimated 480 hours per quarter
of manual reconciliation effort between its e-commerce platform and
warehouse management system.

## Proposed approach

We proposed a 16-week engagement:

- **Discover (3 weeks):** end-to-end process mapping of the
  order-to-fulfilment journey across online and in-store channels,
  quantification of the reconciliation burden.
- **Design (3 weeks):** target-state process design and workflow-automation
  specification for the platform-to-warehouse reconciliation gap.
- **Deliver (8 weeks):** build and test the automation, phased store-by-store
  rollout sequenced to avoid disruption during trading hours, with each
  cohort of stores validated before the next cohort began.
- **Embed (2 weeks):** handover, training for store and warehouse teams, and
  a hypercare period through the client's next peak trading period.

## Relevant experience

This proposal drew directly on Aldermere's Operations & Process
Transformation practice track record; the resulting engagement is now
documented as Case Study 2, having reduced order-to-fulfilment cycle time
by 35% and cut manual reconciliation effort from 480 to approximately
90 hours per quarter, with no store reporting a fulfilment disruption
during rollout.

## Risk and mitigation

The principal delivery risk identified was disruption to in-store trading
during the phased rollout; this was mitigated by sequencing the rollout
store-by-store rather than as a single cutover, validating each cohort's
performance for one full trading week before proceeding to the next.
